An extension means a player who was already under contract agrees new, longer and richer terms before he can reach free agency. Seattle have done that with a cornerback who has been picked for the Pro Bowl three times, at a reported four years and $132 million. In practice they have decided he is a foundation piece and paid the price required to stop anyone else bidding for him later.

For a Bills supporter the direct effect is small — Seattle are in the other conference and this is not a move Buffalo were competing in. The indirect effect is the one to file away: every large cornerback deal resets what the next good cornerback expects to be paid, and Buffalo will be having those conversations with their own defensive backs sooner or later.
