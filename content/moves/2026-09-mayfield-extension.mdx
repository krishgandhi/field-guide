An extension simply adds new years and new money to a contract before the old one runs out. Tampa Bay have done that with their quarterback for three years and $165 million, which locks in both the player and a large chunk of what they can spend on everyone else. Notably, Mayfield had said in public that he wanted a deal done by a certain point in the calendar, and the agreement arrived after he had let that idea go.

For a Buffalo fan the direct effect is nil — Tampa Bay sit in the other conference and would only be met in a Super Bowl. The indirect effect is the one to watch: every big quarterback agreement becomes the number the next quarterback's agent points at, and Buffalo's own payroll planning lives in that world too.
